About me

I am Karim Hamdan, a Lebanese student at Beirut Modern School. For as long as I can remember, I have wanted to understand how things actually work: engines, cars, machines, and the systems behind the technology we use every day.

In January 2022, when I was twelve, I lost my father, a dentist, to a sudden heart attack. Since then, my mother has carried our family on her own. Watching her work long days without complaint taught me what responsibility really means, and it is the reason I take my education and my future seriously.

Model United Nations shaped me as a speaker and leader. Today my focus is on building myself as an engineer: teaching myself new technical skills and working on quite a few projects of my own.

Why mechanical engineering

Engines, vehicle dynamics, and robotics have always fascinated me, not as things to use, but as systems to understand and eventually build. Mechanical engineering is where curiosity turns into real solutions: physics, math, design, and hands-on problem solving working together.

There is also a quiet rivalry in my choice. My father was a doctor who understood the human body. I want to be an engineer who understands machines. Different fields, the same drive to master something difficult and use it to help people.

Why LAU

The first time I walked onto LAU's campus, I was not a visitor. I was a delegate. At the 20th Global Classrooms LAU Model United Nations Conference in 2025, I spent days debating, negotiating, and defending ideas alongside students from across Lebanon, and my school later recognized my performance with a Certificate of Achievement. But what stayed with me was not the certificate. It was the feeling that I belonged in that environment.

At LAU I saw a place where ambition is normal, where students are pushed to think beyond their circumstances, and where Lebanon's future is taken seriously, even in difficult times. That matters to me. My family has been through hard years, and so has my country. I do not want to leave to build my future somewhere else. I want to build it here, and one day help build Lebanon's future too.

I already know what it feels like to walk into LAU as a delegate. Now I want to walk in as a student, and one day walk out as an engineer.
